## Why We're Confident About the "Expected" Dates

JEE Main's scheduling has been remarkably consistent for three straight cycles. Here's the actual pattern, session by session:

| Cycle | Session 1 Registration | Session 1 Exam | Session 2 Registration | Session 2 Exam |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **2025** | Oct 28 – Nov 22, 2024 | Last week of January 2025 | Jan 31 – Feb 24, 2025 | Apr 1 – Apr 8, 2025 |
| **2026** | Oct 31 – Nov 27, 2025 | Jan 21 – Jan 30, 2026 | through Feb 25, 2026 (reopened to Mar 13) | Apr 2 – Apr 8, 2026 |
| **2027 (Expected)** | Late Oct – Nov 2026 | Last week of January 2027 | Late Jan – Feb 2027 | First 1–2 weeks of April 2027 |

Every single cycle: notification in October, Session 1 registration opens within days of it, Session 1 exam lands in the last week of January, Session 2 exam runs in the first eight days of April. That's the basis for every "expected" date on this page — not a guess, an extrapolation from a pattern NTA has held for three years running.

> [!NOTE]
> One wrinkle worth knowing: JEE Main 2026's Session 2 registration reopened briefly (Feb 25 → Mar 13, 2026) after initially closing. NTA doesn't explain these reopenings in advance, but they've happened before — don't panic if you miss what looks like the final date, and don't rely on one either.

## JEE Main 2026: What Just Happened (Useful Context for 2027)

The just-completed cycle sets the bar for what 2027 aspirants are up against:

- **A record 15.5 lakh+ unique candidates** appeared across 19 shifts (10 in January, 9 in April) — the highest ever for JEE Main.
- **General category cutoff for JEE Advanced eligibility: 93.41 percentile** — up from 93.10 (2025) and 93.24 (2024), the highest in four years. Roughly 2.5 lakh candidates qualified for JEE Advanced.
- **April (Session 2) was the easier session** — 99th percentile needed about 177 marks in April versus roughly 162 in January. Session 2 has trended easier than Session 1 for several years running, which is one reason serious aspirants attempt both sessions rather than banking everything on January.
- NTA uses the **better of your two session percentiles** for the final merit list — there's no downside to attempting both.

None of this predicts exactly where the 2027 cutoff will land, but the direction has been consistently upward for four years. Plan your preparation assuming the bar keeps rising, not holding steady.

## Eligibility for JEE Main 2027

- Must have passed Class 12 (or equivalent) with Physics and Mathematics compulsory, plus Chemistry/Biology/Biotechnology/a technical vocational subject as the third. Class 11/12-appearing candidates in the relevant academic year are also eligible.
- **There is no minimum Class 12 percentage required just to sit the exam.** That requirement only kicks in at admission.
- **For admission to NITs, IIITs, and GFTIs specifically:** you need either 75% aggregate in Class 12 (65% for SC/ST/PwD), **or** a rank in the top 20 percentile of your own board's results that year. Either route is valid — the top-20-percentile option exists precisely because board difficulty and grading vary, and it's the one many students forget to check before assuming they're ineligible.
- No upper age limit. NRI/OCI/PIO/foreign nationals are eligible.
- **Maximum 6 attempts total**, spread across 3 consecutive years (2 sessions per year).

> [!TIP]
> The "75% or top-20-percentile" rule gets rediscovered as a rumor every single year ("Is it removed for 2027?"). It isn't, and it hasn't been removed in any recent cycle — this checks out independently across NTA's own information bulletins going back several years. Plan around it rather than hoping it disappears.

## Exam Pattern — Including One Change Worth Flagging

If you're studying from material older than 2025, this section matters more than usual.

**Paper 1 (B.E./B.Tech.) — 75 questions, 300 marks:**

| Section | Physics | Chemistry | Mathematics |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Section A (MCQ) | 20 questions, all compulsory | 20 questions, all compulsory | 20 questions, all compulsory |
| Section B (Numerical) | 5 questions, all compulsory | 5 questions, all compulsory | 5 questions, all compulsory |

> [!WARNING]
> **The Section B rule changed starting JEE Main 2025 and carries into 2027.** It used to be 10 numerical questions per subject with a choice of any 5 — full flexibility to skip the two you weren't sure about. That choice is gone. All 5 are now compulsory, **and they now carry the same -1 negative marking as MCQs for a wrong answer** (previously, numerical answers had zero penalty for a wrong guess). If your prep material or an older previous-year paper describes "10 numericals, attempt any 5, no negative marking" — that's the pre-2025 rule and no longer applies. This alone changes exam strategy: guessing on a numerical question is now exactly as risky as guessing on an MCQ.

Marking scheme: **+4 for every correct answer, -1 for every incorrect answer** (MCQ and numerical alike), 0 for unattempted.

**Paper 2A (B.Arch.) — 77 questions, 400 marks:** Mathematics (20 MCQ + 5 compulsory numerical), Aptitude (50 MCQ), and a Drawing test (2 tasks, pen-and-paper, evaluated separately from the CBT sections).

**Paper 2B (B.Planning) — 100 questions, 400 marks:** Mathematics, Aptitude, and Planning-specific questions, fully computer-based (no drawing component).

You can attempt Paper 2A, Paper 2B, or both in the same sitting (3 hours 30 minutes combined) if you're considering both B.Arch. and B.Planning routes.

## Syllabus: No Changes Expected for 2027

The JEE Main syllabus was significantly trimmed in 2024 to align more closely with the NCERT Class 11–12 curriculum (roughly 15% of topics removed from the pre-2024 version). It has held stable since, and no revision has been announced or is expected for 2027. **The 2026 syllabus is safe to study from today** — don't wait for an official 2027 syllabus PDF before starting.

**Physics:** Kinematics, Laws of Motion, Work/Energy/Power, Rotational Motion, Gravitation, Properties of Solids & Liquids, Thermodynamics, Kinetic Theory of Gases, Oscillations & Waves, Electrostatics, Current Electricity, Magnetic Effects, Electromagnetic Induction, Electromagnetic Waves, Optics, Dual Nature of Matter, Atoms & Nuclei, Electronic Devices, Communication Systems.

**Chemistry:** Atomic Structure, Chemical Bonding, States of Matter, Thermodynamics, Equilibrium, Redox Reactions, Electrochemistry, Chemical Kinetics, Surface Chemistry, Periodic Properties, Hydrogen, s/p/d/f-block Elements, Coordination Compounds, and the full Organic Chemistry sequence through Biomolecules and Polymers.

**Mathematics:** Sets/Relations/Functions, Complex Numbers, Quadratic Equations, Sequences & Series, Permutations & Combinations, Binomial Theorem, Matrices & Determinants, Calculus (Limits through Differential Equations), Coordinate Geometry, 3D Geometry, Vectors, Statistics & Probability, Trigonometry.

## How to Register (Based on the 2026 Process — Expected to Carry Over)

1. **Visit jeemain.nta.nic.in** once the 2027 registration link goes live and complete the one-time registration with basic personal details.
2. **Fill in academic and category details**, matching your Class 10/12 marksheets exactly.
3. **Upload your photograph and signature** per the specifications in the information bulletin — this is the single most common cause of application rejection or correction-window scrambling.
4. **Choose your paper(s)** — Paper 1, 2A, 2B, or a combination. Even if you only intend Session 1, note that registering also gives you the option to apply for Session 2 later.
5. **Pay the fee online** — no cash, cheque, or demand draft is accepted. For India centres: General category is ₹1,000 (male) / ₹800 (female) for a single paper; SC/ST/PwD/Transgender candidates pay ₹500. Fees roughly double for multiple papers, and are substantially higher for centres outside India. The fee is non-refundable except for duplicate or failed transactions.
6. **Download and save your confirmation page.**

A correction window typically opens for a few days after registration closes, allowing limited edits to photograph, signature, and some application details — but not category or date of birth.

## What's Actually Being Discussed for 2027 (and What Isn't)

Two pieces of genuine news are worth knowing, both administrative rather than structural:

- **NTA introduced a four-tier question-paper checking system** and restructured its examination team (reportedly removing around 600 experts and inducting new specialists), aimed at reducing errors and ambiguous questions. This is a quality/security process change — there's no indication it's intended to make the exam harder, and difficulty will only be clear once the 2027 papers are actually administered.
- **A "One Nation, One Entrance Exam" proposal** — merging JEE Main and NEET-style exams into a single national test — has been discussed by a Parliamentary Standing Committee. It remains at the discussion stage with no executive order. For 2027, plan around JEE Main and NEET continuing as separate exams, as they always have.

Treat both as background information, not a reason to change how you're preparing right now.

## A Simple Preparation Framework While You Wait for the Notification

**Now through the notification (this month or two):** Finish a first full pass of the syllabus if you haven't. Since the syllabus isn't changing, this time isn't wasted waiting — it's exactly when serious aspirants build their foundation, before test-day logistics start eating attention.

**Once registration opens:** Register promptly for Session 1 rather than waiting. It costs nothing to register early, and it removes one source of last-minute stress from your plan.

**October–December:** Shift toward timed practice — full-length mocks under actual CBT conditions, with the Section B compulsory-and-negative-marking rule factored into your attempt strategy from day one, not discovered on exam day.

**After Session 1 (~late January):** Use your Session 1 result to calibrate, not to panic or coast. Since NTA takes your better session score, a strong Session 1 buys flexibility; a weaker one makes Session 2 preparation the priority, not an afterthought.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When will JEE Main 2027 registration open?
Not yet announced. Based on the last three cycles, expect the notification around late October 2026, with Session 1 registration opening within days of it — likely late October to mid-November 2026.

### Has the JEE Main exam pattern changed for 2027?
No new changes are expected beyond what already took effect in 2025 (5 compulsory numerical questions per subject, with negative marking now applied to them). If you're studying from pre-2025 material, that's the one change to correct for immediately.

### Is the JEE Main 2027 syllabus different from 2026?
No. The syllabus was last revised in 2024 and has been stable since, with no changes announced or expected for 2027.

### How many times can I attempt JEE Main?
Up to 6 times total, across 3 consecutive years (2 sessions each year). NTA considers your best percentile across all attempts for admission purposes.

### What was the JEE Main 2026 cutoff, and what might 2027 look like?
The General category cutoff for JEE Advanced eligibility was 93.41 percentile in 2026, up from 93.10 in 2025 and 93.24 in 2024 — a fourth consecutive year of increases, driven by rising candidate numbers. There's no official projection for 2027, but the direction has been consistently upward.

### Do I need 75% in Class 12 to write JEE Main?
No — there's no minimum percentage to sit the exam itself. The 75% rule (or top-20-percentile alternative) only applies when you're seeking admission to an NIT, IIIT, or GFTI through JoSAA counselling.
