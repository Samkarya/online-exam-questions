## What Is NECO SSCE Internal?

The **Senior School Certificate Examination (SSCE) Internal** is the exam conducted every June/July by the National Examinations Council (NECO) for students in their final year of senior secondary school (SS3) in registered schools across Nigeria and overseas. It's the "school candidate" route — distinct from **SSCE External**, commonly called **NECO GCE**, which is for private candidates writing in November.

The SSCE Internal offline and online registration system is a suite of software that lets each school (referred to as a "centre") register its candidates, make payment, and manage candidate information for the exam. Through the portal, a school can:

- Create and manage an admin account for the centre
- Make payment for validated candidates
- Upload candidates' information and generate exam numbers
- View registered candidates and print photocards
- Upload candidates' continuous assessment scores

*Source: NECO SSCE Internal User Guide*

## Who Is Eligible to Register?

According to NECO's official 2026 guidelines, **only candidates in their final year of Senior School (SS3)** are eligible for SSCE Internal registration. A few points worth underlining for schools and parents:

- The 2026 SSCE Internal is strictly for **school-based candidates** — explicitly **not** open to private candidates (who register separately for the GCE/External exam instead).
- **Admission or transfer of candidates into SS3 across schools is prohibited.** A student cannot be moved into SS3 at a new school shortly before the exam purely to sit NECO there — schools and States Ministries of Education are required to enforce this.
- Candidates with Special Needs are registered the same way as everyone else, but their specific need must be correctly indicated during registration (see the dedicated section below).

*Source: NECO 2026 SSCE Internal Registration Guidelines, Section 1.0*

## Step-by-Step: How Schools Register Candidates

Registration for SSCE Internal candidates is handled entirely by the school, not by individual students. It's a combination of **offline** data capture and **online** upload, done through NECO's dedicated portal at **ssceinternal.neco.gov.ng**.

### 1. Claim or access the school's online portal
- **Existing schools** continue using their existing login details. If there's a login problem, use the "forgot password" option, or the "centre dispute" process if the portal is genuinely inaccessible.
- **New/fresh schools** must "claim" their portal first, using the school's **official email address and phone number**. Personal or cybercafé emails are explicitly discouraged — a school without an official email is required to create one. Once the claim form (school number, admin full name, email, phone, school type, address) is submitted and approved, an invitation email with login credentials is sent to the official school email.

### 2. Install and use the offline registration application
- Schools download the 2026 SSCE Internal **offline application** from `neco.gov.ng/exams/ssce-internal` (any previous year's offline app should be uninstalled first).
- Launch the app, enter the school number, and click "Proceed" to download the school's details (requires an internet connection at this stage).
- Log in to the app using the admin username (registered email) and password.

### 3. Capture each candidate's bio-data
For each candidate, the school enters (via "Add New Candidate" / "Registration"):
- Country, Quota Type
- Surname, First Name, Middle Name
- **NIN (National Identification Number)** — 11 digits, compulsory
- State of Origin, Local Government Area
- Gender, Date of Birth
- Special Needs category, if applicable

### 4. Select subjects
- Three core/compulsory subjects — **English Language, General Mathematics, and Civic Education** — are auto-selected by the system.
- The school then selects an **additional 6 or 5 subjects** (to reach a minimum of 8 and maximum of 9 subjects total), which **must include at least one Trade/Entrepreneurship subject**.
- CA1 and CA2 (continuous assessment) scores are entered for every selected subject.

### 5. Capture the candidate's passport photograph
- The photo must be taken in the candidate's **school uniform**.
- Technical spec from the portal: maximum size 60kb, with dimensions between 250px (minimum) and 450px (maximum).

### 6. Offline validation
- After data entry, the school prints an offline **photocard** for each candidate showing their NIN, name, passport photo, state of origin, LGA, date of birth, gender, and subjects, so the candidate can personally verify every field.
- Any errors found are corrected **at this offline stage only** — corrections after the data is uploaded online trigger the "non-validation" penalty described below.
- Once corrections are done, the school prints the offline **Validation List**, and every candidate signs against their own name.

### 7. Biometric data capturing
- All **10 fingerprints** of each candidate are captured using a **Futronic (FS80H)** fingerprint scanner as part of offline registration — without this step, a candidate's data cannot be uploaded.
- **No one may capture fingerprints on behalf of another candidate.** If a candidate's biometric data doesn't match what was uploaded, they're treated as impersonating another candidate and denied entry into the exam hall.
- Duplicate registration isn't possible, since each candidate's biometric signature is unique.

### 8. Submit validation documents to the State Coordinator
Before a school can pay and upload candidates, it must submit the following to its State Coordinator, who then activates the centre for payment:
- The signed offline Validation List
- Candidates' signed photocards
- The Entry Schedule, endorsed by the State Ministry of Education (as applicable)
- The Subject Analysis

Centre Activation for the 2026 cycle commenced **Monday, 26 January 2026**.

### 9. Make payment
- All payments go into the **NECO Treasury Single Account (TSA)** via the **Remita** platform, ideally through the school's online portal, using one of six methods: **Card, Bank Branch, USSD, Internet Banking, Wallet, or eNaira**. Bank transfers should be avoided.
- After paying, the school verifies the payment on the portal by clicking "Verify Bank Payment" and entering the 12-digit **Remita Retrieval Reference (RRR)** number.
- **If paying in installments, the initial payment must cover at least 20 candidates**, to avoid being charged the unviable centre fee.
- Once payment succeeds, a **Registration Quota** is allocated matching the amount paid, which the school then uses to select which candidates to upload (individually, or via "select all" if paying for everyone at once).
- The signed RRR e-receipts, payment summary, and the final signed Validation List (now carrying candidates' registration numbers) must be submitted immediately to the State Office.

### 10. Upload candidates and print final photocards
- On the "Upload" section of the portal, the school logs in with admin credentials, selects the quota to use, ticks the candidates to upload, and clicks "Start Uploading."
- Once a candidate's upload is complete, the school can go to "Candidates," select the candidate, click "View Photocard," and then "Print Photocard" to produce the **final photocard**, which every candidate is required to carry for identification throughout the examination period.

> [!NOTE]
> Editing a candidate's record is only possible **before** validation and upload. Once a candidate has been validated and uploaded, no further changes are possible through the normal process — see "non-validation" below for what happens if a correction is still needed.

*Source: NECO 2026 SSCE Internal Registration Guidelines; NECO SSCE Internal User Guide*

### What "Non-Validation" Means (and why it's expensive)

NECO treats any request to correct a candidate's **Name, Passport Photograph, Date of Birth, Gender, Subject(s), State of Origin, or Local Government Area** *after* the data has already been uploaded online as a case of **"non-validation."** This carries a flat penalty of **₦10,000 per candidate**, no matter which field needed correcting. A few specific rules apply:

- **Passport photograph** can only be changed before the end of the registration period — not after.
- **Name and passport photograph** cannot both be changed — only one of the two can be corrected, and only before the end of the registration period.
- Corrections to **gender, date of birth, subjects, state, LGA, or letters within a name** can be entertained up until the end of the examination itself (still subject to the non-validation fee).

The takeaway for schools is simple: get every field right during offline validation, *before* upload — it's free at that stage and expensive afterward.

*Source: NECO 2026 SSCE Internal Registration Guidelines, Section 3.6*

## Candidates With Special Needs

Candidates with Special Needs go through the same registration process as everyone else, but their specific need must be accurately recorded in the offline application. NECO's official guidelines list the recognised categories as:

- Low Vision
- Complete Blindness
- Albinism
- Hearing Impairment
- Autism *(this one requires official notification to the Council)*

On exam day, this classification matters practically: the official 2026 timetable confirms that **albino and blind candidates are entitled to 30 extra minutes at the end of each subject**, and **blind candidates must use the NECO-supplied OBR customised Braille sheet**.

*Source: NECO 2026 SSCE Internal Registration Guidelines, Section 3.3; NECO 2026 SSCE Internal Timetable notes*

## CBE vs Pen-and-Paper: The 2026 Dual-Mode Exam

The 2026 SSCE Internal is officially a **dual-mode exam**, comprising a hybrid of **Computer-Based Examination (CBE)** and the traditional **Pen-and-Paper Examination (PPE)**. NECO's guidelines specify that:

- Schools would be individually notified of whether they're being assessed via CBE, PPE, or a mix, ahead of the examination.
- Schools that already have CBE facilities are required to ensure those facilities meet the requirements for a CBE centre, as published on the NECO portal.

This reflects the broader national push (directed by the Federal Ministry of Education from 2025) for both WAEC and NECO to gradually shift toward full computer-based testing, starting with objective papers. For 2026, most candidates nationwide still sat the majority of their papers on paper, with a growing number of CBE pilot centres.

*Source: NECO 2026 SSCE Internal Registration Guidelines, Section 2.0*

## Continuous Assessment (CAS) Rules for 2026

There's a notable policy change for 2026, driven by new Federal Ministry of Education guidance:

- Schools are **no longer required to upload Third Year Continuous Assessment Scores (CAS 3)**.
- Instead, Continuous Assessment Scores are now uploaded **termly, for SSS1 and SSS2 only**, on the following schedule:
  - **First Term:** January
  - **Second Term:** April
  - **Third Term:** August
- Strict adherence to these timelines is required — schools that fail to comply are subject to sanctions.

*Source: NECO 2026 SSCE Internal Registration Guidelines, Section 8.0*

## Exam Day Rules Every Candidate Must Know

The official timetable document closes with twelve notes every candidate, parent, and school should be aware of:

1. **A photocard is mandatory** for every candidate, for identification throughout the examination.
2. If the time printed on the **question paper** differs from the time on this published timetable, the time on the question paper takes precedence.
3. For Physical Education Practical (Paper I), and French/Music/Arabic Paper IV, the **specific venue** should be confirmed directly from NECO State Offices.
4. Science and Computer Studies practicals may run **in sets/shifts** where a centre has a large number of candidates and equipment is limited.
5. The Visual Arts Paper IV (Creative Design) topic must be given to candidates **at least one week before** the exam date.
6. Nigerian Language papers (**Hausa, Igbo, Yoruba**) include a **literature component**.
7. **Albino and blind candidates get 30 extra minutes** at the end of each subject.
8. **Blind candidates** must use the **NECO-supplied OBR customised Braille sheet**.
9. Use of a **scientific calculator is allowed**.
10. **Electronic communication gadgets are strictly prohibited** in the exam hall — explicitly including programmable calculators, mobile phones, iPads, tablets, smart watches, smart glasses, and even smart pens.
11. **Candidates who don't sit for the Practical (Paper I) will not receive a result for that subject** — this applies across all practical-based subjects.
12. **Trade subject** questions are **hybrid in nature** (a mix of formats).

*Source: NECO 2026 SSCE Internal Examination Timetable, closing notes*

## Subjects: The Internal-Specific Rules

Every candidate registers for a **minimum of 8 and maximum of 9 subjects**, always including the three compulsory core subjects — **English Language, General Mathematics, and Civic Education** — plus at least one **Trade/Entrepreneurship subject**, as confirmed both by the registration guidelines and the subject-selection screen in the offline app. Trade subjects span a wide range of technical/vocational options, several of which also appear directly on the 2026 timetable — including Catering Craft, Fashion Design and Garment Making, Beauty and Cosmetology, Computer Hardware and GSM Repairs, Livestock Farming, Marketing, and Technical Drawing, among others.

For the full stream-by-stream subject breakdown (Science, Arts, Commercial), what's inside the syllabus for each subject, the grading system, malpractice/withheld-result rules, and how to verify a certificate — all of which apply the same way to Internal candidates — see our companion guide, [*NECO Syllabus, Subjects, Grading, Verification & More: The Complete Reference Guide*](/blog/neco-syllabus-grading-verification-guide).

**Practical tip:** subject choice should ideally be finalised by SS2, driven backward from the university course a student intends to pursue — check the specific JAMB/university subject requirements for that course before locking in a combination.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**When does NECO 2026 SSCE Internal registration close?**
Normal registration closed Monday, 25 May 2026, with a late registration window running 26–29 May 2026 (portal shut 11:59 pm on 29 May). There was no extension.

**Is NECO 2026 registration open to private candidates?**
No. The 2026 SSCE Internal is strictly for school-based SS3 candidates. Private candidates register separately for the SSCE External (GCE), which for 2026 runs its own registration cycle from July to November.

**How much is the NECO 2026 registration fee?**
₦30,000 per candidate, inclusive of a Four-Figure Mathematical Table and a Waterproof Certificate Jacket/Folder, plus stamp duty, service, and Remita charges. The syllabus is a separate ₦2,000 per copy.

**Is NIN compulsory for NECO 2026?**
Yes. Every candidate is required to have a valid National Identification Number (NIN) as part of registration.

**When is the NECO 2026 SSCE Internal exam?**
Monday, 15 June 2026 to Thursday, 23 July 2026.

**Is NECO 2026 fully computer-based (CBT)?**
No — it's a dual-mode exam combining Computer-Based Examination (CBE) at select centres with traditional Pen-and-Paper Examination (PPE) at others. Schools were notified in advance which mode applied to them.

**What happens if a candidate doesn't sit a practical paper?**
They will not receive a result for that subject at all, regardless of their performance on the theory papers.

**What subjects are compulsory for every NECO candidate?**
English Language, General Mathematics, and Civic Education, plus at least one Trade/Entrepreneurship subject.

**Can a school correct a candidate's name after registration is uploaded?**
Only one of name or passport photograph can be corrected, and only before the end of the registration period — and it incurs a ₦10,000 "non-validation" penalty. Other fields (gender, date of birth, subjects, state, LGA) can be corrected up until the end of the exam, still subject to the same fee.

**When will NECO 2026 SSCE results be released?**
NECO's Registrar has stated results are expected by the first week of September 2026, or earlier.

**Is BECE the same as NECO SSCE?**
No — BECE is the separate junior secondary exit exam for JSS3 students (mostly run by state Ministries of Education), while NECO's SSCE covers SS3 students only.
