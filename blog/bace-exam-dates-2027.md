Unlike a lot of exams, the BACE doesn't have one single "test day" you can circle on a calendar. It runs two separate tracks: schools and colleges register as **Exam Sites** and operate on a fixed academic-year calendar, while **individual candidates** using the Remote Testing Option (RTO) can register and test any day of the year. If you're planning around "when can I take the BACE in 2027," the honest answer depends entirely on which track you're on — so this page covers both, plus what Biotility has actually confirmed so far for AY 2026–2027 versus what we're projecting from the last few years' pattern.

## Two Calendars, Not One

### If You're Testing Through a School or College (Exam Site)

Your school or program has to register as a Biotility Exam Site *every single academic year* — even if it registered last year — and that registration has to happen before anyone can order exams. The rough shape of a cycle looks like this:

1. **Exam Site Registration opens** (this is the September date everyone watches)
2. **First-attempt orders open**, generally about a month after registration opens
3. **First-attempt testing window** runs through the school year
4. **Retake orders and retake testing** close out the cycle, usually in mid-to-late summer
5. **Registration closes** for the outgoing year around the same time the next year's registration is about to open

If you're a student, none of this is something you personally file — your instructor or site administrator handles registration and ordering. What it means for you practically: ask your instructor in **September, not April,** whether your school has re-registered for the year, since a site that forgets to re-register can't order your exam later.

### If You're Registering on Your Own (Remote Testing Option)

None of the above applies to you. RTO registration is **on-demand, year-round**, through [UF's Continuing Education portal](https://reg.pwd.aa.ufl.edu/search/publicCourseSearchDetails.do?method=load&courseId=1908292#courseSectionDetails_1913127). The only clock that matters is the one that starts when *you* enroll:

- **First attempt:** 180 days to complete once enrolled
- **Second attempt:** 90 days
- **Third attempt:** 90 days
- **Mandatory gap between attempts:** 20 days minimum (Biotility recommends 4–6 weeks)

So if you're asking "when should I plan to take the BACE in 2027," and you're going the RTO route, the real answer is: whenever you're ready. There's no season to wait for.

## Fees and Ordering Lead Times (Quick Recap)

These haven't changed for AY 2026–2027 as of publication, and they apply regardless of which cycle you're in:

- **Exam Site:** $185 per candidate, covering the first attempt plus one free retake.
- **Remote Testing Option:** $200 for the first attempt (includes one free retake), $50 for a third attempt.
- **Site order lead times:** six weeks' notice for first attempts, two weeks' for retakes.
- **Change fees:** free within 14 days of placing an order, $175 after that, or $425 to expedite an order placed with less than six weeks' notice.

Full breakdown, including what's included in the fee and how attempts are tracked, is in the [Complete BACE Study Guide](/blog/bace-exam-guide).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When does BACE Exam Site Registration open for 2026–2027?
September 21, 2026 — confirmed directly from Biotility's official calendar.

### Why did registration open later this year than in past years?
Biotility hasn't published a reason. The pattern for the previous three cycles was registration opening in the first three weeks of September; this cycle's September 21 date is later than that pattern would suggest. Don't assume a fixed date without checking each year.

### Can I still take the BACE right now, before September 21?
If you're going through the Remote Testing Option, yes — that registration path is open year-round and isn't affected by the Exam Site calendar at all. If you're relying on a school Exam Site, your options depend on whether your site has leftover capacity from the current cycle; ask your instructor.

### When will the full AY 2026–2027 testing calendar (testing windows, retake deadlines) be available?
Alongside the September 21, 2026 registration opening, when Biotility publishes the updated Candidate Information Bulletin and Exam Specifications for the new cycle.

### Is the exam content or format changing for 2026–2027?
Nothing has been announced. Biotility reviews and updates the BACE annually with its National Advisory Board, so expect routine refinements rather than a structural overhaul — the 116-question, 175-point, 80%-to-pass format has been stable for multiple cycles.

### Should I use this page or the main study guide to prepare?
Both, for different purposes. This page tells you *when* things happen. The [Complete BACE Study Guide](/blog/bace-exam-guide) tells you *what* to actually study — all 8 exam categories, the math you need to nail, and a full study plan.
