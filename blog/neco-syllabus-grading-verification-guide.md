## What Is NECO?

The <abbr title="National Examinations Council">NECO</abbr> is a Nigerian government examination body headquartered in Minna, Niger State. It was established in April 1999 under former Head of State General Abdulsalami Abubakar, created to give Nigeria a second, more affordable national examination body alongside the older <abbr title="West African Examinations Council">WAEC</abbr> — partly in response to the sheer, growing number of candidates each year. The first NECO exam was held in 2000.

NECO actually runs several examinations, though the SSCE is what most people mean when they simply say "NECO":

| Exam | Who sits it | Registration route |
|---|---|---|
| **SSCE Internal** | SS3 students in registered schools | Through the school |
| **SSCE External (GCE)** | Private candidates | Individually, via cybercafé/online |
| **BECE** *(NECO's own version)* | Federal Unity Colleges, Armed Forces Secondary Schools | Through the school |
| **NCEE** | Final-year primary pupils seeking entry into Unity Colleges | Through the school |
| **National Gifted Examination** | Identifying academically gifted students | Through the school |

> [!NOTE]
> This one trips up a lot of people: the "BECE" most Nigerian JSS3 students sit is run by state Ministries of Education, not NECO. NECO's own BECE, listed above, is a separate and much smaller exam for a specific group of federal schools.

*Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Examination_Council_(Nigeria)*

## The NECO Syllabus: What It Is and Why It Matters

The NECO syllabus is the official document listing every topic, subtopic, and learning objective a candidate is expected to have covered before sitting a subject. It's built directly on the Nigerian secondary school curriculum, and <mark>every single question NECO sets comes from this document</mark> — nothing outside it.

Here's the thing most students get backwards: they prepare using past questions alone, and never actually open the syllabus. That's riskier than it sounds. Past questions only show *what has already been asked* — the syllabus shows *the full range of what can be asked*. Use both together: syllabus first to map out the scope, past questions second to practise against real exam conditions.

**What using the syllabus properly gives you:**
- A clear boundary of what to study — and what you can safely skip
- The ability to plan a realistic study timetable, topic by topic
- Less exam-day anxiety, since nothing on the paper should be unfamiliar in scope

## What's Inside the Syllabus, Subject by Subject

NECO's subjects fall broadly into five streams: **Core/Compulsory**, **Science**, **Arts/Humanities**, **Commercial/Business**, and **Trade/Vocational**. Here's a snapshot of what the syllabus actually covers for the most commonly written subjects.

### Core subjects

**English Language** — comprehension and summary, lexis and structure (grammar/vocabulary), essay writing (formal/informal letters, narrative, descriptive, argumentative), and oral English (vowels, consonants, stress, intonation).

**Mathematics** — number and numeration (fractions, indices, logarithms, standard form), algebraic processes (equations, inequalities, sequences), geometry and trigonometry, statistics and probability, and basic calculus (differentiation/integration).

**Civic Education** — citizenship and civic responsibility, democracy and rule of law, human rights and national values, drug abuse and social vices, and civic participation/leadership.

### Science stream

- **Biology** — cell biology, genetics and evolution, ecology, human physiology, plant biology, and practical biology (microscopy, specimen identification, dissection).
- **Chemistry** — atomic structure, chemical bonding, acids/bases/salts, organic chemistry, electrochemistry, and chemical calculations (mole concept, stoichiometry).
- **Physics** — mechanics, heat and temperature, waves, electricity and magnetism, modern physics (radioactivity, photoelectric effect).
- **Further Mathematics** — sets/surds/indices, permutations and combinations, binomial theorem, advanced calculus, vectors and matrices.
- **Agricultural Science** — crop science, animal science, farm management, soil science, and practical farm work.
- **Computer Studies** — computing/hardware basics, software, internet/networking, spreadsheets/databases/word processing, and introductory programming concepts.

### Arts/Humanities stream

- **Literature in English** — African prose fiction, African and non-African poetry, drama, oral literature, and critical appreciation, drawn from NECO's approved list of set texts for the cycle.
- **Government** — the state and sovereignty, Nigerian government and constitutional history, international relations (UN, AU, ECOWAS), and public administration.
- **History** — pre-colonial Nigerian societies, the slave trade and colonialism, Nigerian nationalism and independence, and post-colonial African politics.
- **CRS/IRS** — CRS covers Old and New Testament content including the life of Christ and Paul's missionary journeys; IRS covers the life of the Prophet, the Qur'an, and Islamic worship/law.
- **Nigerian languages (Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa)** — grammar and vocabulary, comprehension, essay/letter writing, and literature in the language.

### Commercial/Business stream

- **Economics** — basic concepts (scarcity, opportunity cost), demand/supply, market structures, national income, and international trade.
- **Commerce** — trade concepts, commercial documents (invoice, receipt, waybill), banking/insurance/transport, and advertising/marketing.
- **Accounting** — book-keeping, trial balance, trading/profit-and-loss accounts, balance sheets, depreciation, and partnership accounts.
- **Marketing** — marketing concepts, market research, product/pricing/distribution strategy, and consumer behaviour.

## NECO Subject List and Combinations

The number of subjects and the exact subject list differ between the two NECO exams:

- **SSCE Internal** candidates register for a minimum of 8 and maximum of 9 subjects, always including English Language, General Mathematics, and Civic Education, plus at least one Trade/Entrepreneurship subject — see our Internal guide for the full breakdown, trade options included.
- **SSCE External (GCE)** is limited to a fixed list of just 16 subjects for the 2026 cycle, with no trade/vocational options at all — see our External guide for the complete list.

**General pattern by stream** (applies mainly to Internal, where the full subject range is available):

| Stream | Typical core electives | Common career paths |
|---|---|---|
| Science | Physics, Chemistry, Biology, (Further Maths/Agric optional) | Medicine, Engineering, Computer Science, Pharmacy, Nursing |
| Arts | Literature in English, Government, History, CRS/IRS, a Nigerian language | Law, Mass Communication, Political Science, Linguistics |
| Commercial | Economics, Commerce, Accounting, Marketing/Business Studies | Accounting, Banking & Finance, Business Administration |

**Practical tip for parents and students:** work backward from the intended university course. Check the JAMB/university's specific subject requirements *before* finalising a combination — a Science student missing one required elective can limit admission options later, and that's a mistake that's painful to fix after the fact.

## The NECO Grading System

| Marks | Grade | Remark |
|---|---|---|
| 75–100 | A1 | Excellent |
| 70–74 | B2 | Very Good |
| 65–69 | B3 | Good |
| 60–64 | C4 | Credit |
| 55–59 | C5 | Credit |
| 50–54 | C6 | Credit |
| 45–49 | D7 | Pass |
| 40–44 | E8 | Pass |
| 39 and below | F9 | Fail |

This scale applies identically to **both SSCE Internal and SSCE External**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Most Nigerian tertiary admissions require **at least 5 credits (C6 or above)**, including English Language and Mathematics, achieved in a maximum of two sittings. That two-sitting rule is exactly why so many candidates use a GCE sitting to top up an earlier Internal result.

*Source: [syllabus.ng/neco](https://syllabus.ng/neco)*

## Exam Malpractice, Withheld and Cancelled Results

NECO has formal rules for handling malpractice, and understanding how the process actually works can save you from mistaking a "withheld" result for a lost one.

- **Withheld/held results** usually mean NECO's Special Committee on Malpractice is reviewing a case linked to the candidate or their centre. If cleared, the result is released later; if not, it can be cancelled.
- **Cancellation of Subject Result (CSR)** applies to individual subjects implicated in malpractice; **Cancellation of Entire Results (CER)** applies when a candidate is found guilty more broadly.
- **Centre de-recognition** can happen where mass cheating is established at a centre.
- NECO uses **biometric verification** — both at registration and, increasingly, during the exam itself — along with candidate-specific answer booklets, specifically to curb impersonation and proxy registration. A biometric mismatch at any stage is treated as impersonation and can bar a candidate from the exam hall entirely.

> [!CAUTION]
> For **SSCE External** specifically, registering more than once results in the candidate's **entire result being cancelled** — regardless of which subjects were duplicated.

If a result is withheld, the recommended first step for Internal candidates is to contact the school where the exam was written, since the school liaises with NECO on the candidate's behalf. Resolution can take several weeks, so it's worth being patient rather than panicking.

## NECO e-Verify: Confirming a Certificate Is Genuine

For employers, schools, and institutions that need to confirm a candidate's SSCE result is authentic, NECO operates a dedicated portal called **NECO e-Verify** (`everify.neco.gov.ng`), launched in 2023. It lets individuals, employers, and institutions instantly authenticate a candidate's result and academic information — cutting down significantly on forged certificates.

- Individuals can verify a single result through public access.
- Institutions and employers can register for bulk or API-based verification.
- This works for results from **both** SSCE Internal and SSCE External.

Certificates themselves also carry embedded anti-fraud features: the candidate's registration photograph is embossed and their <abbr title="National Identification Number">NIN</abbr> is inscribed directly onto the physical certificate.

*Source: everify.neco.gov.ng; Guardian Nigeria*

## Study and Preparation Tips

**For students:**
- Treat the syllabus as your primary study document, not past questions alone — read it fully once before you start topic-by-topic revision.
- Confirm your subject combination against your target university course early, not at registration time.
- Keep your registration slip, photocard, and result-checking token safe. Without them, you simply cannot access your result.

**For parents:**
- Verify registration deadlines and fees directly with the school (Internal) or the official portal (External), rather than relying on social media posts — secondary sources often report conflicting dates.
- If a result comes back withheld, don't assume the worst. Contact the school first (Internal) or check NECO's official channels (External); most cases resolve within weeks once reviewed.

**For teachers and school administrators:**
- Encourage syllabus-first revision planning over past-question-only preparation, especially for Science practicals — the compulsory practical paper is one many students under-prepare for.
- If a student is considering a GCE sitting to top up results, confirm early whether their required subject is actually on the current GCE list. It's shorter than most people assume.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What's the difference between NECO SSCE Internal and External?**
Internal is for current SS3 students, registered through their school, running in June/July. External (GCE) is for private candidates, registered individually, running in November. See our dedicated guides for each for the full details.

**Is BECE the same as NECO?**
No. The BECE most JSS3 students sit is administered by state Ministries of Education, not NECO. NECO's SSCE — the exam most people mean by "NECO" — is for SS3 students only.

**What is NECO's grading system?**
A1 (75–100, Excellent) down to F9 (39 and below, Fail), with C6 (50–54) as the lowest credit grade. Most universities require at least 5 credits including English and Mathematics.

**How do I verify that a NECO certificate is genuine?**
Use the NECO e-Verify portal at `everify.neco.gov.ng`, which lets individuals, employers, and institutions instantly confirm a result's authenticity.

**What happens if my NECO result is withheld?**
It usually means your case is under review by NECO's Special Committee on Malpractice — not necessarily that something is wrong. Contact your school (Internal) or NECO directly (External) for next steps; resolution can take several weeks.

**Does the NECO syllabus change every year?**
The core structure is generally stable, but always download the current cycle's syllabus rather than relying on an old copy — specific set texts (e.g., for Literature in English) and minor topic emphases can be updated.
