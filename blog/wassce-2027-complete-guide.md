## Why Nobody Has 2027 Dates Yet — And Why That's Normal

If you're searching for a "WAEC 2027 timetable," stop and check the date on whatever page you're reading. As of this update, WAEC has not published a final timetable for the 2027 May/June WASSCE, and multiple education-news sites confirm the same thing: the latest official Nigerian timetable in circulation is still the 2026 one.

That's not unusual. WAEC's pattern in recent years has been to release the final school-candidate timetable roughly two to four months ahead of the exam window, once registration numbers and centre allocations are locked in. For the GCE series, the pattern is tighter still — the 1st Series (Jan/Feb) timetable typically appears only weeks before candidates sit, right after registration closes.

**What this means practically:** don't plan your calendar around a specific 2027 exam date you found on a blog. Plan around the syllabus and subject rules instead — those are already locked in, and that's most of what determines how well you do.

## What's Actually Confirmed for 2027

Several things, directly from WAEC's own country-specific guidance, hold for both the 2026 and 2027 cycles:

### 1. Nigeria: it's 8 subjects minimum, not 7

This is worth stating plainly because it's a genuine, sourced correction to something that circulates widely online (including, until this update, in our own 2026 guide): WAEC Nigeria's entry regulations require a **minimum of eight (8) and a maximum of nine (9) subjects**, not seven. If you're a Nigerian candidate and you've budgeted your subject list around "7 minimum," add one more before you register.

### 2. Ghana: the rule is genuinely different, not a rounding error

Ghanaian candidates work from a different structure entirely, per WAEC Ghana's own guidance (waecgh.org): **4 core subjects** (English, Core Mathematics, Integrated Science, Social Studies) plus **3 or 4 electives** chosen from a single WAEC-defined programme option — Science, Business, General Arts, Agricultural, or Vocational — for a total of **7 to 8 subjects**. If you're Ghanaian, use this range, not Nigeria's 8–9.

### 3. Nigeria's two core subjects stay the same

English Language and General Mathematics remain the only two compulsory core subjects for Nigerian candidates registering in 2027. You then choose six or seven electives to reach the required total of eight or nine.

### 4. Two new subjects are still not examinable

Citizenship and Heritage Studies and Digital Technologies — both introduced under West Africa's revised senior-secondary curriculum — will **not** be examined until **2028**. WAEC has said both need their curricula and syllabuses fully implemented first. If you're a 2027 candidate, don't register for either; they're simply not on the table yet.

Everything else about the syllabus and paper structure — Paper 1 (Objective), Paper 2 (Essay/Theory), Paper 3 (Practical/Oral/Alternative-to-Practical) — carries over unchanged from 2026. If you want the full subject-by-subject topic breakdown and Chief Examiner-sourced preparation tactics, that lives in our [WASSCE 2026 guide](/blog/wassce-2026-official-updates-cbt-dates-fees-guide) rather than repeated here, since none of it has changed for 2027.

## The Fee Question — Genuinely Open, and Worth Understanding

This is the part of the 2027 picture that's actually different from anything in the 2026 cycle, and it deserves more than a one-line disclaimer.

**What happened:** WAEC requested an increase to its base registration fee, and in a memo dated **June 18, 2026**, Nigeria's Federal Ministry of Education approved raising it from **₦27,500 to ₦50,000** — an 82% jump — effective from the **2027 examination cycle**. NECO's internal SSCE fee was set to rise in parallel, from ₦30,000 to the same ₦50,000 figure. The Ministry cited rising costs of printing, logistics across thousands of centres, security, and examiner remuneration as the justification.

**What happened next:** the announcement drew immediate, sustained public criticism — from parents' groups, the National Association of Nigerian Students, and public figures including former Vice President Atiku Abubakar, who framed the increase as a threat to secondary-school access for lower-income families. On **July 13, 2026**, the Federal Ministry of Education withdrew the June letter entirely, stating the pause was meant to allow "broader consultations with relevant stakeholders" before any final decision.

**Where it stands now:** unresolved. The Ministry has not announced an interim fee, a revised figure, or a timeline for when a decision will be made. That leaves a real gap for anyone budgeting ahead: the old ₦27,500 rate isn't officially reconfirmed as the 2027 fee, and the ₦50,000 rate isn't dead — it's paused.

**What to actually do about it:** don't pay anything based on a number you saw in a September 2026 blog post, including this one. When WAEC opens 2027 registration, the fee will be stated on the official portal (waeconline.org.ng) and in WAEC's own registration announcements. Budget with some flexibility until that happens, and treat a figure anywhere between the two as plausible rather than confirmed.

## The Event That Actually Matters Next: GCE 2027 1st Series

While the May/June 2027 school-candidate exam is still far off, there's a nearer-term WASSCE sitting worth planning for now: the **GCE 1st Series**, typically held in **January–February**.

Based on the last two cycles' pattern:

| Cycle | Registration Window | Exam Window |
|:---|:---|:---|
| 2026 1st Series | Closed Jan 27, 2026 | Jan 28 – Feb 14, 2026 (CBT) |
| 2026 2nd Series | Extended to Aug 30, 2026 | ~Oct 25 – Dec 20, 2026 (CBT) |
| **2027 1st Series (expected)** | **Likely opens Oct/Nov 2026** | **Likely late Jan – mid-Feb 2027** |

If this pattern holds, registration for the 2027 1st Series could open within weeks of this update — private candidates who want to sit in January/February 2027 should start watching WAEC's official channels (waeconline.org.ng and WAEC's verified social accounts) from October 2026 onward, rather than waiting for a January announcement.

Format-wise, the GCE has been fully computer-based for objective papers since the 2026 cycle, with a hybrid approach (on-screen questions, handwritten answers) for essay and practical papers. There's no indication this reverts for 2027.

## Grading, At a Glance

No changes are expected here for 2027 — WAEC's 9-point scale has been stable for years:

| Grade | Range | Meaning |
|:---|:---|:---|
| A1–B3 | 65–100% | Excellent to Good |
| C4–C6 | 50–64% | Credit (C6 = minimum credit) |
| D7–E8 | 40–49% | Pass (not a credit) |
| F9 | 0–39% | Fail |

Most Nigerian and Ghanaian tertiary institutions require five credits at C6 or above, including English Language and Mathematics, for admission. That threshold has held for years and there's no signal it's changing for 2027.

## How to Actually Use the Waiting Period

Waiting for a timetable is not the same as waiting to prepare. A few things worth doing between now and whenever WAEC publishes 2027 dates:

- **Lock in your subject list against your country's confirmed rule now** — 8–9 for Nigeria, 7–8 for Ghana — rather than after registration opens, so you're not scrambling to adjust at the last minute.
- **Download the current official syllabus for each subject** from waeconline.org.ng using a registration PIN — syllabus content doesn't wait for a timetable, and it's the same document that will govern the 2027 exam unless WAEC announces otherwise.
- **If you're a GCE candidate, get comfortable with CBT now**, using WAEC's own past-CBT interface style or established practice platforms — this has been the format for two consecutive GCE cycles and shows no sign of reverting.
- **Track the fee decision separately from your study plan.** It affects your budget, not your syllabus — don't let fee uncertainty distract from actual preparation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Has WAEC announced WASSCE 2027 exam dates?**

No. As of this update, no official 2027 timetable has been released for either the May/June school-candidate exam or the GCE 1st Series. Treat any specific date you see elsewhere as unconfirmed.

---

**Is the minimum really 8 subjects, not 7?**

For Nigeria, yes — WAEC Nigeria's own eligibility guidance states a minimum of eight and maximum of nine subjects, for both the 2026 and 2027 cycles. Many third-party guides — including older versions of content on this site — still say seven for Nigeria, which is incorrect. Ghana's rule is different again: 4 core subjects plus 3–4 electives, for a 7–8 total. Apply the figure for your own country.

---

**Will the WAEC fee actually go up to ₦50,000 for 2027?**

Unknown. A hike to ₦50,000 was approved by the Federal Ministry of Education in June 2026, then suspended in July 2026 after public backlash, with no replacement figure announced since. Don't budget with certainty around either the old or the proposed new figure until WAEC or the Ministry confirms one.

---

**Can I register for Citizenship and Heritage Studies or Digital Technologies in 2027?**

No. WAEC has confirmed neither subject will be examinable before 2028, regardless of when your school's curriculum introduces them.

---

**When should I start watching for the 2027 GCE 1st Series registration?**

Based on the 2026 pattern, registration for a January/February sitting tends to open in the last quarter of the prior year. If you want to sit the GCE 1st Series in early 2027, start checking WAEC's official channels from October 2026.

---

**Has the syllabus changed for 2027?**

Not that WAEC has announced. The subject list, core-subject rule, and paper structure (Objective / Essay / Practical) carry over from 2026 unchanged, aside from the confirmed delay of Citizenship and Heritage Studies and Digital Technologies to 2028.

---

**Is there a fee for verifying my result once I have it?**

For Nigerian candidates using their result for university admission, yes — a separate one-off ₦4,000 verification fee (via the JAMB Verification Service) was introduced in September 2026, replacing multiple per-institution charges. This applies after you have a result, not during 2027 registration itself. Full detail is in our [WASSCE 2026 guide](/blog/wassce-2026-official-updates-cbt-dates-fees-guide), since it's current policy regardless of which cycle you sat.
